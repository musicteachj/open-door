## What this policy covers

Juniper Row does not allow harassment or discrimination at work, at work events, or in work-related messages, calls, and online spaces. The policy protects employees, applicants, contractors, and customers, and it applies to everyone, including managers and executives.

## What harassment looks like

Harassment is unwelcome behavior connected to a protected characteristic, such as race, sex, religion, age, or disability, that makes work hostile or intimidating. Examples include:

- slurs, jokes, or nicknames about who a person is
- unwanted touching, comments about a person's body, or repeated invitations after a no
- sharing offensive images or messages
- threats, or offering a benefit in exchange for something personal

## What discrimination looks like

Discrimination is treating someone worse in pay, scheduling, discipline, promotion, or other terms of work because of a protected characteristic. It can be one decision or a pattern.

## You do not have to go through your manager

You can take a concern about harassment or discrimination straight to HR. You never have to tell your manager first, and you do not have to be sure that a rule was broken. Say what happened, and HR takes it from there. See [§2.3 Raising a concern](handbook:2.3).

## No retaliation

Juniper Row forbids retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith or takes part in an investigation. Retaliation includes changing someone's schedule, duties, or treatment because they spoke up. Retaliation is itself a reason for corrective action.

## What happens after you report

HR looks into every report promptly, listens to the people involved, and keeps the matter as private as it can. If you raise it in Open Door by sending a confidential case, only HR sees it. See [§7.2 How confidential HR cases work](handbook:7.2). People, not software, make every decision about the outcome.
