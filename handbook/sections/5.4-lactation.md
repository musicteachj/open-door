## Who this covers

This section applies to every employee who is nursing, hourly or salaried, at every location. It covers you for as long as you are nursing, whatever your child's age. When you come back from parental or medical leave and are still pumping, the same paid breaks and private space are ready for you.

## Paid lactation breaks

You can take break time to express breast milk at work as often as you need to, and each break lasts as long as you need. Lactation breaks are paid at your regular rate. Hourly employees stay clocked in, and salaried employees' pay is never reduced. You never have to use PTO or sick leave for these breaks. They come on top of the rest and meal breaks in [§3.3 Meal and rest breaks](handbook:3.3), and you never have to make up the time.

## A private space

Juniper Row gives you a clean, safe, private space close to your work area. It is never a restroom. It is shielded from view and free from intrusion while you use it, and it has a chair, a surface for a pump and your things, and access to electricity. A sink with running water and a refrigerator for storing milk are nearby.

## How to ask

Tell your manager or HR that you need lactation breaks. You do not need a doctor's note or any other paperwork. Your manager and HR work with you on a space and a schedule that fits your shift, and you can change it whenever your needs change.

If a break or a space can't be provided as this section describes, HR tells you why in writing and works with you on a fix.

## You are protected

No one may hold it against you that you asked for or took lactation breaks. If you think you are being treated worse for it, tell HR. See [§2.3 Raising a concern](handbook:2.3).

## What federal law says

Federal law gives nursing employees break time and a private space other than a bathroom for up to one year after the child's birth. Juniper Row's rule goes further: the breaks are paid, and there is no time limit.

## State rules

California adds its own rights, including extra pay if a break or a space is denied. If you work in California, see [CA-5.4 California lactation](handbook:CA-5.4). Where a state rule is more generous to you, it applies.
