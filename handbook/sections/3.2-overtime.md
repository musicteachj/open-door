## Who gets overtime

This section applies to hourly employees. Juniper Row classifies hourly employees as non-exempt, so they are paid overtime, and salaried roles as exempt from overtime, as described in [§1.2 Employment types](handbook:1.2).

## The basic rule

Under federal law, non-exempt employees must be paid overtime at time and one-half their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Juniper Row follows this rule at every location. The workweek is Sunday through Saturday, as set out in [§3.1 Schedules and timekeeping](handbook:3.1).

## Approval, and pay either way

Overtime should be approved by your manager in advance where possible. If you must stay late for an urgent task, tell your manager as soon as you can. All overtime worked is always paid, whether or not it was approved. If you work unapproved overtime, your manager may talk with you about planning your time, but you are still paid for every hour.

## What counts as hours worked

Time spent working counts toward overtime, including paid rest breaks. Unpaid meal breaks, when you are fully relieved of duty, do not count. Under Juniper Row's policy, paid time off and holidays are paid but don't count as hours worked for overtime.

## State rules

California and Colorado have daily overtime rules that can give you more than the federal rule. If you work in either state, see [CA-3.2 California overtime](handbook:CA-3.2) or [CO-3.2 Colorado overtime](handbook:CO-3.2). Where a state rule is more generous to you, it applies.

## Questions about your pay

If your paycheck does not include overtime you believe you earned, tell HR. We review it promptly and correct any underpayment on the next payday.
