## The federal law, and why most of us are not covered by it

The federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) gives eligible employees up to 12 workweeks of leave in a 12-month period. An employer is covered when it has 50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks in the current or previous calendar year. Juniper Row has more than 50 employees, so it is a covered employer.

## Eligibility under the federal law

To be an eligible employee under the FMLA, you must have worked for the employer at least 12 months, have at least 1,250 hours of service in the 12 months before the leave, and work at a location where the employer has at least 50 employees within 75 miles.

## Where Juniper Row stands

None of Juniper Row's four locations has 50 employees within 75 miles. Denver HQ has 40 people, the Oakland store has 45, the Austin warehouse has 35, and the Atlanta store has 25, and they are in four different states. Most of us are therefore likely not eligible for federal FMLA leave.

## Juniper Row's own leave

Juniper Row offers its own family and medical leave on similar terms. It is a company benefit and does not carry federal FMLA protections. You can take up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period if you have worked at Juniper Row for 12 months and for 1,250 hours in the 12 months before the leave begins.

## Reasons for leave

You can use company family and medical leave for:

- your own serious health condition
- caring for your spouse, child, or parent who has a serious health condition
- the birth or placement of a child
- qualifying military family needs

## Pay and health coverage

Your health coverage continues during leave on the same terms as when you were working. Leave under this section is unpaid, but you may use PTO or sick leave to be paid during it. See [§4.1 Paid time off](handbook:4.1) and [§4.3 Sick leave](handbook:4.3).

## State law

State law may give you more than this section. The California and Colorado addenda cover state leave rights. See [CA-5.1 California family and medical leave](handbook:CA-5.1) and [CO-5.1 Colorado family and medical leave](handbook:CO-5.1). Where a state rule is more generous to you, it applies. For paid parental leave, see [§5.2 Parental leave](handbook:5.2).

## How to ask

Tell HR as early as you can, and at least 30 days ahead when you can plan for the leave. HR explains the steps and any forms.
