## What an accommodation is

An accommodation is a change to your job, schedule, equipment, or workplace that lets you do your work or follow a religious practice. Juniper Row provides reasonable accommodations for disability, pregnancy-related conditions, and sincerely held religious beliefs and practices. For paid breaks and a private space to express breast milk, see [§5.4 Lactation](handbook:5.4).

## Examples

- a different schedule, or a modified shift
- a stool, an adjusted work station, or other equipment
- time off for medical appointments
- changes to lifting or standing duties during pregnancy
- a schedule change for a religious observance, or an adjustment to a dress or grooming rule

## How to ask

You can ask HR for an accommodation, and you do not have to go through your manager. You can also tell your manager, and your manager passes the request to HR. You do not need to use special words or have a diagnosis ready. Saying that you need a change because of a medical condition, pregnancy, or your faith is enough to begin.

## The conversation

HR talks with you to understand what you need and what is getting in the way. This is an interactive conversation, and we look at options together. HR may ask for a note from a health care provider about what you need at work. The note does not need to include more than that.

## Decisions and follow-up

HR works with you and your manager to choose a change that works for you and for the business, and tells you the decision in writing. If a requested change is not possible, HR looks for an alternative with you. You can ask HR to review the plan again any time your needs change.

## Privacy

HR keeps medical information separate from your regular personnel record. Your manager is told only what they need to carry out the change, such as the change itself, and not your diagnosis. See [§7.1 Your personal information](handbook:7.1).

## No retaliation

Juniper Row forbids retaliation against anyone who asks for an accommodation or takes part in the process. If you think you are being treated worse for asking, tell HR. See [§2.3 Raising a concern](handbook:2.3).
