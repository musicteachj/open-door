## How much you receive

Juniper Row gives every employee 40 hours (5 days) of paid sick leave each year. This is the company rule for any state that has no sick leave law of its own, which today includes Texas and Georgia. Sick leave is separate from paid time off, so using it never reduces your PTO. See [§4.1 Paid time off](handbook:4.1).

## When you receive it

The 40 hours are front-loaded, which means you receive them all at once on January 1 each year. A new hire receives 40 hours on their first day of work.

## What you can use it for

You can use sick leave for:

- your own illness or injury, or to get medical care
- caring for a family member who is ill or needs care
- medical appointments for you or a family member
- time off related to domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking, for you or a family member

## Notes and notice

You do not need a doctor's note for absences of up to three consecutive scheduled days. For a longer absence, HR may ask for a note confirming that you needed to be away, and the note does not need to describe your condition. The California and Colorado addenda have their own documentation rules, which apply to you instead. Tell your manager as early as you can when you will be out, and record the time in Open Door.

You can use sick leave in whole hours, from 1 hour up to a full scheduled day, or in whole scheduled days. Sick leave doesn't need approval: record it in Open Door and it counts at once, and your manager can see it. You can record sick time up to 30 days after the day you were out, and cancel recorded time until its first day.

Sick time can't be recorded for a day before your first day of work, or for a day after December 31 of next year.

## What happens to unused hours

Unused sick hours do not carry over into the next year. On January 1, a new 40 hours replace what is left. Unused sick hours are not paid out when your employment ends.

## State addenda may give you more

A state addendum may give you more than the company rule. If you work in California, see [CA-4.3 California sick leave](handbook:CA-4.3). If you work in Colorado, see [CO-4.3 Colorado sick leave](handbook:CO-4.3). Where a state addendum is more generous to you, it applies in place of this section.

## Longer illness

If you need more time than your sick leave covers, you may be able to use PTO, or take a leave of absence. See [§5.1 Family and medical leave](handbook:5.1).
