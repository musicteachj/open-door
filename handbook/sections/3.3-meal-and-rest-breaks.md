## Who this covers

This section applies to hourly employees. Salaried employees manage their own breaks in line with their role.

## Rest breaks

Hourly employees get a paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked. Your rest break is paid time, so you do not clock out for it. Take it away from your work station if you can, and let your manager know when you are stepping away.

## Meal breaks

Hourly employees get an unpaid, uninterrupted 30-minute meal break for any shift over 5 hours. Your meal break must start before the end of your fifth hour of work. You clock out for the meal break and clock back in when it ends.

## Fully relieved of duty

During your meal break, you are fully relieved of duty. You do not have to answer calls, help customers, or watch the floor. If you are asked to work during your meal break, tell your manager, and the time is paid as work time.

## What federal law says

Federal law does not require employers to give meal or rest breaks. When an employer gives short breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes, the breaks are paid work time. Meal periods of at least 30 minutes, when the employee is fully relieved of duty, are not work time. Juniper Row's rules above meet or go beyond these federal rules.

## State rules

California and Colorado have their own meal and rest rules. If you work in either state, see [§CA-3.3 California meal and rest periods](handbook:CA-3.3) or [§CO-3.3 Colorado meal and rest periods](handbook:CO-3.3). Where a state rule is more generous to you, it applies.

## If a break is missed

If you could not take a break, tell your manager and note it in the timekeeping system. HR reviews missed breaks and works with managers to schedule coverage so that breaks can be taken. For how time is recorded, see [§3.1 Schedules and timekeeping](handbook:3.1).
