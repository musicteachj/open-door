This addendum applies to employees working in Georgia, including everyone at the Atlanta store. It adds to [§4.5 Jury duty and voting](handbook:4.5). Where the Georgia rule or the company rule is more generous to you, the more generous one applies.

## Court attendance is job-protected

Georgia law protects your job when a court requires you to attend. If you are absent to answer a jury summons, a subpoena, or another court order, you cannot be fired, disciplined, or otherwise penalized for it, or threatened with any of these. The protection does not cover an employee who is charged with a crime.

Juniper Row may ask for reasonable notice. Tell your manager as soon as you receive a summons, subpoena, or order, and say when you expect to be away or late.

## Pay for jury service

Juniper Row pays you for jury service: your full regular pay for up to 10 working days a year, under [§4.5 Jury duty and voting](handbook:4.5). That pay does not reduce your PTO. For other court attendance, such as answering a witness subpoena, ask HR how your time is paid.

## Time to vote

In Georgia, you can take up to 2 hours off to vote in each primary or election, whatever your schedule. Juniper Row pays you for that time. The 3-hour test in the company section does not apply to you. You can use the time on Election Day or on one of the advance in-person voting days.

Give your manager reasonable notice, and at least 2 working days when you can. Your manager may set which hours you take, so the store stays covered. Juniper Row pays for the time, and it does not reduce your PTO.

## Questions

Ask HR if you are unsure how these rules apply to your schedule, or if you are called to court more than once in a year.
