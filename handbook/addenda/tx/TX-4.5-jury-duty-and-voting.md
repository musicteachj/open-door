This addendum applies to employees working in Texas, including everyone at the Austin warehouse. It adds to [§4.5 Jury duty and voting](handbook:4.5). Where the Texas rule or the company rule is more generous to you, the more generous one applies.

## Jury service is job-protected

Texas law protects your job while you serve on a jury. You cannot be fired or face any other adverse action for serving. Please tell your manager as soon as you receive your summons, as described in the company section, so your schedule can be planned.

## Pay for jury service

Texas law generally does not require an employer to pay you for jury service. Juniper Row pays you anyway: you receive your full regular pay for up to 10 working days of jury service in a year, under [§4.5 Jury duty and voting](handbook:4.5). That pay does not reduce your PTO.

## Time to vote

Texas law entitles you to paid time off to vote on Election Day, unless you have at least two consecutive hours to vote outside your working hours. It does not apply if you have already voted early.

Juniper Row's company rule gives you up to 2 hours of paid time off to vote if you do not have at least 3 consecutive hours outside your working hours while the polls are open. Request it before Election Day, and at least 2 working days ahead when you can. The time is paid and does not reduce your PTO.

## Questions

Ask HR if you are unsure how these rules apply to your schedule, or if you are called for service more than once in a year.
